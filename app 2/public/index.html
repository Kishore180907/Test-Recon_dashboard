Customer journey

 

How to read this

Five minutes, in plain English. Every picture below is a real screenshot of this page — the numbers in them are sample data, not your store.

1 What you are looking at

Every online order your store took, sorted by what actually made the sale happen. Not where the money came from — who or what closed it.

  • Ecommerce The customer found you, shopped and checked out on their own, through any of your digital channels — your website, the Shop app, StockX, Facebook & Instagram, Marketplace Connect or Shopify Mobile. Nobody from your team touched it.
  • Assisted Someone helped. Either a staff member is credited on the order note, or the customer came in through an ad or a marketing email before buying.
  • Draft Written up by hand in Shopify and sent to the customer as an invoice — and the customer arrived on their own, with no ad or campaign in their history.
In-store sales are not in here. Anything rung up on the POS is counted separately in the grey strip under the tiles, so it never inflates these three numbers.

2 Three things people usually come here to find out

  • “How much of last week did the team actually sell?” Click 7 days, then read the Assisted tile. The share underneath tells you what portion of revenue that was.
  • “Is our ad spend doing anything?” Open a bucket and look down the First click and Last click columns. Orders that started at facebook, ig or Google were reached by advertising, whoever closed them.
  • “Which of us closed the big orders this month?” Click MTD, open Assisted, then click the Net sale column heading to sort largest first. The orange chip under each order number is the staff member credited on it.

3 Choosing your dates

The presets cover most of it. For anything else, set From and to and press Apply. You can go back 90 days; older than that is not kept.

Exclusive and Overlay change one thing. Exclusive is the normal view: every order sits in exactly one tile, so the three add up to your total. Overlay lets an assisted order appear in its own tile and in Online or Draft, which is useful for spotting overlap but means the tiles deliberately add up to more than the total.

Refresh now pulls straight from Shopify instead of waiting for the next scheduled check. Pause stops the page updating under you while you are reading it.

4 The daily chart

Use it to spot the shape of a week rather than the detail: a Tuesday that is almost all Draft, a weekend that is almost all Online. A single very tall bar is usually one large invoice, and clicking through to the table will show you which.

5 Opening a tile to see the orders

Click any tile and the table below fills with the orders behind that number.

  • First click How the customer first found you, which can be weeks before they bought. The grey line underneath is the page they landed on, or the campaign name.
  • Last click Where they came from on the visit they actually bought on.
  • Traffic source The last click again, plus how many separate visits it took and how many days from first visit to purchase.
  • Converted via How the sale physically closed — a normal storefront checkout, the Shop app, or a draft invoice link you sent them.
  • The violet ad spend chip Meta was genuinely being billed for that campaign during these dates. It is the difference between a link that merely says “facebook” and money actually being spent.

“No journey data” is not a fault. Shopify simply never resolved where that customer came from, which happens with app orders and some repeat customers.

6 Assisted orders, and who gets credit

Two different things land an order here, and it helps to know which.

  • A staff credit Someone wrote a credit note on the order in Shopify. That person's name shows in the orange chip.
  • A marketing touch on a draft order The order was written up by hand, but the customer had already come through an ad, an email or a referring site. Marketing did part of the work, so the sale is counted as assisted rather than draft.
The rule for drafts, exactly. A hand-written order stays in Draft only when both its first and last touch are Direct. If either one names a marketing or ad platform, it moves to Assisted. An order with no journey data at all stays in Draft — Shopify not knowing where someone came from is not evidence of advertising.

7 What Meta says, next to what your store says

The two sides are matched on campaign name, and they are supposed to disagree. Meta counts people who saw an ad and bought later on another device, or without ever clicking. Those arrive at your store with nothing attached, so Shopify records them as Direct.

That difference is the Gap column. +4 means Meta claims four more purchases than your store credited to that campaign. It is not an error to be fixed — it is the reason to treat some of that week's “Direct” orders as ad-influenced.

A campaign with Meta spend and no Shopify orders at all is the same story in its strongest form. A campaign with orders and no spend simply was not running in these dates.

8 Worth knowing

  • How fresh is this? The store is checked every 15 minutes. The header tells you how long ago that was, and the dot turns grey if it has gone quiet.
  • Whose clock? Every date and time is your store's local time, so “today” means the same thing here as it does in Shopify.
  • What does Net sale include? What you actually kept — refunds are already taken off. Cancelled orders stay listed, marked in red, so the count still reconciles against Shopify.
  • Getting it into a spreadsheet Export CSV hands you everything in the open tile, attribution columns and all.
